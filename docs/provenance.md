# Provenance

Each subsystem is original C++. The notes name the evidence used to get the behavior right. Decompiler listings are not copied in.

## Logon and world session

- Build 8606, little-endian logon body, reversed `x86` / `Win` / `enUS` tags, and the realm-list shape for 2.4.3 were checked against `mangos-tbc` `src/realmd/AuthSocket.cpp` and then against a live realm.
- SRP-6 (N, g = 7, k = 3), the interleaved 40-byte session key, and M1/M2 were checked against `src/shared/Auth/SRP6.cpp`. Unpadded bignum hashing is called out in `discrepancies.md`.
- The world header sizes (6 bytes client to server, 4 bytes server to client), the auth-session digest `SHA1(account | 0 | clientSeed | serverSeed | K)`, and the single HMAC-SHA1 header key were checked against `src/game/Server/WorldSocket.cpp` and `src/game/Server/AuthCrypt.cpp`.
- Opcode names and the session gate on each opcode come from the 2.4.3 opcode map (`Opcodes.hpp`, generated from the public CMaNGOS table). `NUM_MSG_TYPES` is 0x424.
- Update blocks, movement info, speed acknowledgements, and teleport acknowledgements were checked against `src/game/Entities/Object.cpp`, `src/game/Entities/Unit.cpp`, and `src/game/MotionGenerators/MovementHandler.cpp`.
- Ghidra 12.2 headless analysis of `Wow.exe` finished successfully. The project is `wow243` under `D:\home\tools\ghidra-projects` (outside this repo). Addresses for anything that disagrees with the server go in `discrepancies.md`.
- The compatible stack is REA 6.7.0, Ghidra 12.1.4 at `D:\home\tools\ghidra\ghidra_12.1.4_PUBLIC`, and JDK 25. `GHIDRA_INSTALL_DIR` points at that install. The 12.2 tree stays on disk. A separate 12.1.4 project, `wow243114`, holds the same `Wow.exe` (23644 functions, no PDB). Hopper, IDA, JADX, and Kuna are optional engines REA does not install; they are not required for this x86 executable.

## Assets, view, and UI

- MPQ hash, block decrypt, and zlib sectors were checked by reading `FrameXML.toc`, `Map.dbc`, `Azeroth.wdt`, `Azeroth_32_48.adt`, an M2, a BLP, and a WMO group out of the local install. Nine archives open, highest priority first (`patch-2` through `base-enUS`). StormLib is listed in `vcpkg.json` for implode and binary-diff patches; those paths are not required by the files read so far.
- DBC is the WDBC record layout. BLP2 palette images decode to RGBA. M2 MD20 versions 256–264 expose the vertex and first skin-profile index arrays. A character file has no embedded image. The body starts from `CharSections.dbc` (the player's race and face bytes, or `CreatureDisplayInfoExtra.dbc` for a creature), then the face, facial hair, and scalp are painted into the head, and worn items add their component textures from `ItemDisplayInfo.dbc` (`Item.dbc` maps an item entry to that display). Bones play the stand sequence while idle. The player runs by default and plays the run sequence. Numpad 0 toggles walk, which plays the walk sequence. Backward motion plays the backward sequence. His mesh is kept for as long as he wears that model. A skinned pose is copied into a driver buffer at the draw, so the hall and the other people cannot overwrite that step. Every other unit and game object keeps its own animation clock; only the player advances his. Character models and their skins are read and decoded off the draw thread, one at a time, and appear once ready. A step stops at a wall of the city mesh, including one the body is already touching, and slides along it. Turning the camera in a tunnel keeps the view in front of the wall.  Left click selects a nearby creature. A right click asks them to talk, and a right drag still turns the camera. The gossip window is the stock GossipFrame. Greeting and option lines sit in the parchment, and the option buttons call SelectGossipOption. The dialog is drawn over the chat. The account screen draws only the account, password, and character fields. One chat window is kept, on a solid black panel above the action bar, with its General tab on that same panel. Extra chat windows are not drawn behind it. Enter opens the chat edit box, and ChatFrame1 prints say, yell, and channel lines. `/1` talks in the first joined channel. On entering a zone the client joins General, LocalDefense, Trade, and LookingForGroup. Backward motion plays the backward sequence. Other units still use the run sequence when they are moving faster than a walk. Playback follows the distance the body moved through the sequence's own keys. A clothing section the body does not contain keeps the naked limb, or the first section that body does contain, so the legs are not left off the skeleton. The camera reuses its wall test until the eye moves or turns, and a closer hit has to repeat before the orbit moves in. Movement, animation, and that camera release follow the measured frame time, capped so one long frame cannot skip ahead. A steady 144 Hz is a performance goal, not the clock. Each clip uses its own start and end, skinned from the pivot rest pose. A character's body is one-sided, so the inside of the face is not drawn on the back of the head. Shoulder armor is a pair of item models from `ItemDisplayInfo.dbc`, hung on attachment points 6 and 5 and textured with that display's shoulder image. A character already posed for this frame is not skinned again, and the body, hair, and cloak each go out as one draw. ADT `MCVT` heights are placed on the 533.333-yard tile grid. A chunk quad the map marks as a hole is not drawn and is not a floor, so the mountain shell does not cover Ironforge's gate. Outdoor chunks use the tile's ground textures and the slope of the chunk normals. The projection uses `nearclip`, `farclip`, and `horizonfarclip` from `Config.wtf` when that file sets them, otherwise the registered defaults (`nearclip` 0.1, `farclip` 350, `horizonfarclip` 2112). Outdoor sky and fog colors come from `Light.dbc` at the camera and the local clock. The fog distance is the `LightFloatBand` value divided by 36, and the fog color is the clear color. The ground keeps its tileset color. Detail coats are drawn out to that fog distance, and the base layer continues to the far clip. Units and map doodads are drawn out to `DistCull` 500. Ground behind the camera is not submitted. The rock and snow coats on a tile share one draw per texture. A placed model's skin batches use that batch's own texture file, and a second layer on the same triangles is drawn over it, so foliage is not the trunk image. Terrain positions and texture coordinates stay on the device after the frame they are first drawn. The normal block is followed by 13 bytes the size does not count, and the texture layers sit after that pad.  That outdoor ground stays drawn inside a building, and the room floor is kept until it meets the outside ground, so the doorway does not pop.  the Northshire start (`-8949, -132`) falls inside `Azeroth_32_48`. WMO vertices and indices are read from the group file after `MOHD` reports the group count.
- Walkable ground is taken from those same archives. Outdoors it is the ADT heightmap. Indoors it is the WMO group mesh, which is the floor under a city rather than the mountain stored in the heightmap. `D:\home\World of Warcraft\vmaps` is an unpacked extract of that collision, kept as a reference for the mesh layout. The client does not open it. The game’s copy of this geometry is the WMO, M2, and ADT files inside `Data\*.MPQ`.
- The window and input are SDL3. The approved renderer is SDL3 GPU, with D3D12 first and the same shaders ready for Metal. The running window draws through an OpenGL 2.1 compatibility context (depth test, client vertex arrays, BLP textures). That was a way to get this hall onto the GPU without a shader cross-compiler. The SDL3 GPU backend is still the target and has not been swapped in. Placement and materials were fitted to the map files, the screenshots, and the class names below. Decompiler listings stay out of this repo.
- Terrain chunks are `CMapRenderChunk`, `CMapChunkBuf`, and `CMapAreaLow`. Buildings are `CMapObj` / `CMapObjDef`. Placed models are `CMapDoodadDef`, with a separate detail-doodad path (`CDetailDoodadInstAdd`, `DetailDoodad.cpp`) and a batching switch. Visibility is `CWorldOccluder` and `CWorldAntiOccluder`. The device side is `CGxShader`, `CGxTexCache`, `CGxStateBom`, and `CGxuLightLink`. Nameplates are `CGNamePlateFrame` (`NamePlateFrame.cpp`). Login and world widgets are the `CSimple*` types.
- `Interface\FrameXML\FrameXML.toc` and `Bindings.xml` are opened by the same startup function that also checks the FrameXML signature and writes `Logs\FrameXML.log`. `World\Maps\` is the prefix used to open `%s\%s.wdt`. A missing building file falls back to `world\wmo\Dungeon\test\missingwmo.wmo`. Minimap tiles come from `Textures\Minimap` using `map%d_%02d.blp` and `%s_%03d_%02d_%02d.blp`, and the arrow is `Interface\Minimap\MinimapArrow.mdx`. Character and creature meshes come from `CreatureDisplayInfo.dbc`, `CreatureDisplayInfoExtra.dbc`, `CreatureModelData.dbc`, and `CharSections.dbc`. Camera behavior lives in `Camera.cpp`: a normal view and a freelook view, plus zoom in/out. Graphics options named in the binary include `showDetailDoodads`, `showSimpleDoodads`, `showLowDetail`, `showCull`, `maxLOD`, and `pixelShaders`.
- FrameXML is Lua 5.1. A fixture button runs `OnLoad` and `OnClick`. On this install `Interface\FrameXML\FrameXML.toc` loads from the client archives, then each `Interface\AddOns\Blizzard_*` addon whose toc is not marked LoadOnDemand. The `.pub` files in those folders are signatures, not scripts; `LoadAddOn` reads the addon toc from the archives when the stock UI asks for it. The loader supplies the `bit` library and the `this` / `event` globals the 2.4.3 scripts use. An unknown widget method is a no-op, and a file that still fails is recorded and skipped. Widget anchors use the interface axes (+x right, +y up) and the nine anchor points, including a point on one frame attached to a different point on another. `CreateFrame` applies the size of the template it names. The options category list divides the frame height by that button height; without the template the height was 0 and the loop ran until the instruction budget, building about 190,000 buttons. Layout runs when a frame moves, is shown or hidden, or the window size changes. A steady frame does not rebuild that layout and does not upload the status text unless it changed. Every visible character is posed each frame, and the draw keeps a copy of that pose because characters of the same model share one mesh. A building group, and a prop model that has not been seen yet, is read and decoded off the draw thread, then installed on a later frame, so walking into the next part of a city does not stop the frame. The camera tests only the city boxes the view ray enters, and a closer hit has to repeat for a frame before the orbit moves in. Zone music follows the area under the player: the terrain chunk outdoors, and the WMO group indoors (`WMOAreaTable`). A subzone with no music of its own uses its parent zone. Entering an area with different music fades the old tune and plays that area's intro, then its day music, keeping the silence between songs. The same area's ambience bed plays indoors and outdoors. A city whose area row leaves music at 0 still plays the `Zone-<name>` entry when `ZoneMusic` points at that entry. `PlaySound` and `PlayMusic` use `SoundEntries`. Footsteps, spells, and combat sounds are not played. Files are the `.mp3` and `.wav` named there, read from the archives. `Bindings.xml` names `TOGGLEUI`: hide `UIParent` when it is shown, and show it again otherwise. The default key for that command is Alt-Z, and the interface starts visible. `UIParent` and `WorldFrame` fill the window. A left click hits the topmost mouse-enabled frame and runs `OnMouseDown` / `OnClick`; the release runs `OnMouseUp`. Creature and player meshes are chosen from `CreatureDisplayInfo.dbc`, which is loaded with the archives rather than only when the archive set was still empty.
